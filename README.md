# Detection of AI-generated video via the frequency domain

IE 643 course project. Astitva Garg (24B2440), team *mdhkideggimirch*.
Repository: https://github.com/Astitva-Garg/Detection-of-AI-Generated-Video-via-Frequency-Domain
Demo video: https://drive.google.com/file/d/1j2pN5h0CsaIPw2K7vSTBoML7otCmXgHS/view?usp=drive_link

A lightweight detector that decides whether a short clip is **camera-captured** or **generated by a diffusion
model**. It measures (a) the frequency content of each frame (how much fine detail it has) and (b) how that
content **changes from frame to frame**, and feeds 263 such features to a small neural network (an MLP). A
fine-tuned ResNet-18 on raw frames is the strong baseline.

## Results at a glance

AUROC on held-out clips, mean over 5 training seeds. *Seen* = clips from generators used in training; *unseen*
generators were never trained on. The pixel MLP is the comparison: it uses no frequency analysis.

| Model | Seen | ModelScope (unseen) | Wan2.1 (unseen) | 250 public clips (mean) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| MLP, frequency + pixel features (combined) | 0.977 | 0.744 | 0.819 | 0.754 |
| MLP, frequency features | 0.966 | 0.798 | 0.690 | 0.727 |
| MLP, pixel statistics | 0.889 | 0.619 | 0.827 | 0.694 |
| ResNet-18, ImageNet-pretrained, fine-tuned | 0.954 | 0.900 | 0.926 | 0.781 |

The pipeline also trains logistic regression, a linear SVM, LightGBM, a 1D-CNN and a pixel CNN; their results are in
[`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md). The poster, the notebooks and the app show only the MLP and ResNet-18.

What this says, and what it does not:
- On generators seen in training, frequency features beat pixel statistics (0.966 against 0.889; for the final
  trained model the paired gap is +0.079, 95 % interval +0.02 to +0.14).
- On unseen generators the result is partial and uneven: frequency is better on ModelScope, pixel cues on Wan2.1,
  and the pretrained ResNet-18 transfers best but is over 250 times larger (11.2 M against 42 k weights) and cannot
  say which frequencies it used.
- Heavy compression, blur and down-scaling remove the high frequencies the method measures and cost the frequency
  MLP 0.2 to 0.3 AUROC; the pixel MLP loses about 0.02.
- The unseen test sets are small (99 and 57 clips) and all real footage is professional stock video.
- Novelty checks (`novelty.ipynb`, `RESULTS.md`): resizing frames instead of cropping at native resolution costs the
  frequency MLP 0.08 AUROC and leaves the pixel MLP unchanged; shuffling the frames costs the time-only MLP 0.14 and
  the spatial-only MLP nothing; with 36 brand-new prompts the pixel, frequency and combined MLPs lose 0.07 to 0.15 on
  ModelScope (frequency still beats pixel, ResNet-18 barely moves); fusing frequency features with ResNet-18 helps
  our model (+0.06 to +0.08 on unseen generators) but not ResNet-18.

Where each number comes from: [`RESULTS.md`](RESULTS.md). Known issues of the data: [`data/README.md`](data/README.md).

## Try it

**Notebooks (a step-by-step walkthrough, CPU only).** Double-click `run_notebook.bat` for `demo.ipynb` or
`run_novelty_notebook.bat` for `novelty.ipynb` (the first run builds `.venv-app` and installs the packages, a few
minutes), or run `python -m notebook demo.ipynb` in the environment. Run the cells one at a time.
- `demo.ipynb`: a real and a generated clip, their spectra computed live, the spectral drift between frames, the
  features of each clip, the verdict of the pixel, frequency and combined models, pixel vs frequency vs combined on
  the 267 held-out clips, and a check against the stored results. To use other clips, change the two clip IDs in
  section 1 (use test-split clips only: look up an ID in `data/manifest_crop.csv`).
- `novelty.ipynb`: the four extra experiments (resize vs native crop, frame order, prompt-disjoint test, ResNet-18
  late fusion), each with a labelled graph from the stored result file, the script that writes it, and a conclusion.

To run a notebook without opening Jupyter: `python tools/run_demo_notebook.py` or
`python tools/run_novelty_notebook.py` (each saves an executed copy and an HTML page in `demo_output/`).

**Terminal demo (about 2 minutes, CPU only).**

```bash
python -m src.demo_live
```

It runs on real held-out clips and prints every step (outputs in `demo_output/`):
1. shows a real and a generated clip (chosen by a fixed random seed, not by hand);
2. extracts their spectra live (2D spectrum, radial curves, noise-residual curves, spectral drift);
3. traces one clip from frames to 263 features to the verdict of every model, with the top feature contributions;
4. compares pixel-only, frequency-only and fusion models on the same 267 held-out clips, with 95 % bootstrap
   intervals, and checks the live numbers against the stored results (`--retrain` also retrains from the training
   features);
5. `--video <file>.mp4` traces any video you give it from the raw file to the verdict. `examples/` holds 18 held-out clips (3 real, 3 each from five generators) with the shipped model's verdict on each, wrong ones included ([`examples/README.md`](examples/README.md)).

**The app** (double-click `run_app.bat`, or `python app.py`, then open http://127.0.0.1:7860). Upload a video or pick
one of the 18 examples (all held-out test clips) and choose a model: the MLP on frequency + pixel features (default),
on frequency features, on spatial frequency only, on temporal features only, on pixel statistics only, or the
fine-tuned ResNet-18. It shows the verdict, four frames with their 2D spectra, and the frame-to-frame spectral drift;
the Performance tab has the stored test tables. An uploaded video is preprocessed automatically (14 frames, native
256 by 256 centre crop, H.264), so upload the original file and not one you have resized. The first analysis takes
about 10 s (the object detector loads), later ones about a second.

## Data

`data/` is not in git. Its layout, what each folder is, how it was made, sizes and known issues are in
[`data/README.md`](data/README.md). To reproduce from the normalised clips you need the data bundle (the
manifests, `clips_crop/`, the small files of `features_crop/`, the resize variant's manifest and features, the 72 new-prompt clips; 188 MB zip); unzip it **in the project root** (the zip already contains the `data/` folder) and check it:

```bash
python -m src.data.checksums --verify        # compares 1,371 core files with data/SHA256SUMS.txt
```

The dataset in numbers: 432 real clips (Pexels) and 552 generated clips from five generators (LTX-Video,
AnimateDiff, Stable Video Diffusion, ModelScope, Wan2.1), split by source group 70 / 15 / 15; ModelScope and
Wan2.1 are test-only. Every clip is reduced to 14 frames, centre-cropped to 256 by 256 at native resolution and
re-encoded identically, so that real and fake differ only in how they were made.

Two folders hold normalised clips: `clips_crop/` is the dataset and the only one the trained models expect;
`clips/` is the resize variant, used only for the resize-vs-crop test (its frames were shrunk, which removes the fine
detail the frequency features measure, so the frequency models do much worse on it).

## Method in brief

- **Spatial features (113 per frame):** grey frame, mean removed, Hann window, 2D FFT, log power averaged over 48
  rings of equal frequency (a 48-point radial curve), the same curve for the noise residual (frame minus its 3 by 3
  median), spectral slope, share of power beyond 25, 50 and 75 % of Nyquist, centroid, 85 % roll-off, three 8 by 8
  DCT band shares, eight orientation wedges. Mean and standard deviation over 14 frames: 226 features.
- **Temporal features (37):** distance between the radial curves of consecutive frames (L2 and cosine) with mean,
  spread, maximum, autocorrelation and second-order change, drift per frequency band, drift of the scalar features,
  and five features of the noise residual over time. Total **263 frequency features**.
- **Pixel baseline (74)** and **fusion (337 = 263 + 74)**.
- **Models shown on the poster, in the notebooks and in the app:** an MLP (263 inputs, 128, 64, 1; 42,113
  parameters) on the pixel, frequency or combined features (also on the spatial-only and temporal-only parts), and
  ResNet-18 (11,177,025 parameters, all layers fine-tuned) on raw frames. The pipeline also trains logistic regression,
  a linear SVM, LightGBM, a spectro-temporal 1D-CNN (64,817) on the 2 x 14 x 48 curves and a pixel CNN (589,553).
- **Training:** weighted binary cross-entropy (fake weighted by real/fake, about 2), AdamW (learning rate 1e-3,
  1e-4 for ResNet-18; weight decay 1e-4), cosine schedule, early stopping or best epoch on validation AUROC. The
  validation split chooses settings; the test split is never used for any choice.
- **Evaluation:** held-out test, grouped 5-fold cross-validation, 5 seeds, bootstrap intervals, unseen generators,
  six degradations, leave-one-generator-out, metadata-only shortcut check, 250 public clips, generator attribution.

## Known limitations

- The unseen generators reuse the same 60 prompts as the seen ones, so the unseen test clips are content-matched
  to training fakes. The seen test clips share no source video or prompt with training. The prompt-disjoint test
  (36 new prompts) checks this: every model loses 0.07 to 0.15 AUROC there.
- A weak cue survives normalisation: compressed file size separates fake from real at AUROC 0.65 (0.86 for
  ModelScope), partly because generated clips hold more fine detail and motion.
- Real data is professional stock footage. A phone clip re-encoded by an app (for example WhatsApp) can make the
  models disagree.
- The detector misses many fakes from generators it never saw: at the 0.5 threshold the combined MLP flags about
  a third of ModelScope clips and about half of Wan2.1 clips; ResNet-18 flags more.
- Generators ran at reduced resolution and steps to fit an 8 GB laptop GPU.
